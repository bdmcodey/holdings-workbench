Conversion settings
How many issues make a volume — 12 for a monthly with annual volumes, 4 for a quarterly. Written into the 853 beside the caption it describes, and only there: the first level has nothing above it to count against, so a serial numbered by volume alone never takes one. A serial with three levels takes none either, because one number cannot say how many of each make the level above, and the tool would rather say so than pick. var and und are accepted where the number varies or is unknown. An 853 that takes a count is written with first indicator 2, can compress or expand, where it would otherwise be 3, unknown: the count is what compression needs. var and und leave it at 3, as does any other value you have put in the indicator box. Left empty nothing is written, which is what previous versions did — this is a fact about how the serial is published, and a holdings statement does not state it.
The level your library reports holdings at, written into each 863 and matching the record's own Leader/17. It is a statement of practice rather than something that can be worked out from a statement: the same field appears in the MARC documentation marked both ways, because two libraries can hold the same run and report it differently. A record whose Leader/17 says something else is flagged on the Convert step, and left alone for you to change.

Leave a caption blank and the level is written as it came from the statement. A level the statement never captions is written (*) — MARC 21 for “no caption on the piece” — rather than guessed at. Type a caption here to supply one (v., no., pt.); bracket it, [v.], if you want the record to show that you supplied it.
Turned off, only your confirmed patterns convert anything. A statement none of them matches is left exactly as it is, 866 and all.
The standard parser follows MARC 21 and Z39.71 conventions. Where a statement goes beyond them, it normally converts what it can and says what it left out. With this on, such a statement is held instead: nothing is written, its 866 stays, and the log says why. Useful for a collection whose holdings were not written the way the parser expects. Statements your confirmed patterns match are not affected.
Some values are read correctly but have nowhere to go in an 863: a season at one end of a range only, as in (1981-Fall 1983), a ? after a year, a date stated once for several runs. They are marked "Left out to fit MARC" on the record and in the log. Choose here whether such a statement is converted anyway, held with its 866 kept, or whether its whole record is left exactly as uploaded. Unlike strict, this applies to statements your patterns read too.
Searches every record in the file, not the page on screen, and narrows whichever filter is selected rather than replacing it.
Off by default. Keeping the originals lets you run the file through again with different settings, and an ILS that rebuilds 866s from 853 / 863 will replace them anyway.
